    Shaping contract

    Each day, the high block is the half-hours with the highest predispatch residual demand at the nomination run, and the low block the lowest. Settlement = quantity × block hours × (strike spread − realised spread), where realised spread = average spot in the high block − average spot in the low block. Positive = paid to the holder (short the spread).

    Blocks are made of half-hours inside the shaping day (start to end, market time; 28:00 = 04:00 the next morning, the end of the trading day). Each block totals the shaping duration, split into at most the maximum number of windows, each at least the minimum window long. The high block is chosen first; the low block is then chosen from the remaining half-hours.

    A day is settled only if the nomination run, the residual demand and the spot price cover every half-hour of the shaping day. Predispatch runs before about 12:30 only reach 04:00 on the shaping day, so early nominations leave most days unsettled.

    About this tool

    This dashboard tests two contract designs against historical NEM data: a bulk energy contract that settles a wind or solar farm against a regional reference generation profile, and a shaping contract that settles the spread between high- and low-demand periods each day. Both are explored here as designs specified for this analysis; they are not a published term sheet.

    Everything is computed in your browser from pre-built data files, so every input (fixed prices, quantities, caps, block rules) can be changed and the results update immediately. Every chart and table has PNG and CSV buttons; the CSV holds the numbers behind the visual at the current settings.

    Bulk energy contract

    A fixed-for-floating swap whose volume follows a regional reference trace rather than the farm's own output. The farm keeps selling its actual output at spot (its merchant revenue); the contract then pays it the fixed price and charges it the floating price on the reference volume. Because the volume is regional, the farm keeps its own basis risk: weeks when it under- or over-performs its region, or captures a different price, are not hedged.

    Reference trace

    • Basket. Set each 1 January, per region and technology (wind, solar): semi-scheduled units registered by that date, not deregistered, and first generating on or before 1 July of the prior year, so every member has at least six months of operation. Capacity is fixed at its 1 January value.
    • Sent-out trace. In each 5-minute interval, the sum of basket sent-out generation (SCADA, clamped to 0–capacity) ÷ the sum of basket capacity. A unit with missing data drops out of both numerator and denominator for that interval only.
    • UIGF-on-negative trace. The same, except that in intervals with a negative regional price each unit's unconstrained forecast (UIGF) replaces its sent-out. It removes the volume lost to economic curtailment, so the contract volume no longer shrinks when prices go negative.

    Each farm's header shows the years its own unit was in its basket, and links to a CSV of every basket member with the dates used for the six-month rule.

    Settlement and the three approaches

    settlement = trace CF × Q (MW) × (K − floating) × 5/60

    Calculated per 5-minute interval and summed by billing week (Sunday–Saturday). K is the fixed price and Q the contract quantity in MW. A positive amount is paid to the generator. The approaches differ only in the floating price and in which intervals carry volume:

    ApproachFloating priceVolumeEffect
    A · ExposedSpotEvery intervalFull swap. The generator pays the spike above K on reference volume, and receives K − P (more than K) when prices are negative.
    B · Cap + floorSpot clamped to [floor, cap], default [$0, $600]Every intervalLimits the generator's payment in spike intervals to the cap, and stops negative prices increasing its receipts above K − floor.
    C · Cap + knockoutSpot capped at the capZero when spot < $0Spikes capped as in B; negative-price intervals drop out of the contract entirely.

    Each approach can be settled against either trace. The UIGF trace differs from sent-out only in negative-price intervals, which approach C knocks out, so it cannot change C.

    merchant = farm sent-out × max(spot, 0) × 5/60   ·   net revenue = merchant + settlement

    Merchant revenue is floored at zero on the assumption that the farm does not generate into negative prices.

    Shaping contract

    A daily contract on the price spread between the hours of highest and lowest residual demand. It values the ability to move energy from low-demand to high-demand periods (the service storage or flexible load provides), and settles on that spread against a fixed strike.

    How it settles

    • Nomination. At the nomination time on the day before (D-1; default 16:00), the contract fixes the day's high and low blocks from that predispatch run.
    • Residual demand = predispatch total demand − predispatch semi-scheduled solar and wind UIGF: the demand that dispatchable plant has to meet.
    • Realised spread = average spot price over the high block − average spot price over the low block, both measured in half-hours.
    settlement to holder = Q (MW) × block duration (h) × (strike spread − realised spread), per day

    The holder is short the spread: it receives the fixed strike and pays the realised spread, so it gains on days when the realised spread comes in below the strike.

    Choosing the blocks

    • Shaping day. Blocks can only use half-hours between the shaping day start and end (market time; 28:00 is 04:00 the next morning, the end of the trading day).
    • Duration. Each block totals the shaping duration (1, 2, 4, 6 or 8 hours).
    • Windows. Each block may be split into at most the maximum number of contiguous windows, each at least the minimum window long. One window of 4 hours is a single evening peak; two windows of at least 1 hour can capture a morning and an evening peak.
    • Selection. The high block is the feasible set with the highest total residual demand, found exactly. The low block is then the feasible set with the lowest total from the half-hours left, so the two never overlap; either may come first in the day.
    • Comparison bases. The same rule is applied to the predispatch price at nomination (an alternative contract design) and to the actual spot price (perfect foresight: the best spread achievable with these block rules).
    A day settles only if the nomination run, the residual demand and the spot price cover every half-hour of the shaping day. Predispatch runs before about 12:30 only reach 04:00 on the shaping day, so early nomination times leave most days unsettled; the tiles say how many days were skipped and why.

    Risk metrics

    All are computed over the complete weeks (bulk) or settled days (shaping) of the selected period.

    Weekly SDSample standard deviation of weekly net revenue. Counts upside and downside alike.
    Downside SD√ mean(min(x − mean, 0)²): only weeks below the mean contribute, so a few very good weeks don't count as risk.
    P10The 10th-percentile week: 10% of weeks fall below it. With about 50 weeks a year it sits between the 5th and 6th worst week, so it can jump as weeks re-rank.
    CVaR 10%The average of the worst 10% of weeks (fractionally weighted at the boundary). Smoother than P10 and sensitive to how bad the tail is, not just where it starts.
    ParametricMean − 1.28 × SD: the 10th percentile if weekly revenue were normally distributed. Perfectly smooth, but it treats upside as risk and misses skew. It overstates risk when revenue is right-skewed (low contract volume) and understates it when a fat left tail appears (high volume under approach A).
    Earnings-at-risk (EaR)Mean week − the selected tail measure: how far a bad week falls short of a typical one. Choose the measure with the toggle on the table or the chart.

    Data and conventions

    • Source. AEMO NEMWeb: MMSDM monthly archive (dispatch, SCADA, prices, registration, all predispatch runs), with daily reports and the weekly predispatch archive for recent months. Rebuilt nightly.
    • Time. Market time (AEST, UTC+10, no daylight saving). Values are labelled by interval ending.
    • Prices before 5-minute settlement (1 October 2021) are the 30-minute trading price, applied to each of its six 5-minute intervals.
    • UIGF is not published in older dispatch files; dispatch AVAILABILITY stands in where it is missing.
    • First generation is the first interval with at least 1 MW of SCADA in the fetched history. Units already generating when the history starts use their registration date.
    • Billing week is Sunday to Saturday in market time.
    • The footer shows the data source, the latest interval included and when the site was built. A demonstration build on synthetic data says so there.
